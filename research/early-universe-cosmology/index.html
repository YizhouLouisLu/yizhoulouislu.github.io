Early-Universe Cosmology

Small fluctuations in the early universe can leave two very different traces. Rare overdensities may collapse into primordial black holes, while pairs of scalar fluctuations can source gravitational waves. I study what each trace tells us about the original fluctuations.

How often do primordial black holes form?

A region can collapse if it is sufficiently overdense when it re-enters the horizon. The abundance of primordial black holes therefore depends on the rare tail of the density distribution. In a model of Higgs inflation with a noncanonical kinetic term, my collaborators and I found that primordial non-Gaussianity made black hole formation more likely while having little effect on the energy density of the induced gravitational waves.

Which part of the perturbation radiates?

At second order, scalar fluctuations source tensor perturbations of the metric. A change of coordinates can also change those tensor perturbations, which complicates the interpretation of a calculated wave spectrum. We used a radiation boundary condition to select the propagating contribution and studied its energy density at wavelengths well inside the horizon.
