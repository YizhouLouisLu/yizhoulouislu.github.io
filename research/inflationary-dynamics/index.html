Inflationary Dynamics

The fluctuations that seed cosmic structure depend on how fields move and interact during inflation. I study when those interactions amplify fluctuations, how far a perturbative calculation can be trusted, and what information survives in gravitational waves.

When the trajectory turns

With several fields, the inflationary trajectory can turn in field space. In the models we studied, a strong turn can briefly amplify curvature fluctuations. We calculated how those fluctuations feed back into the scalar and tensor power spectra at one loop. Requiring the loop corrections to remain under control constrains how much scalar power these models can produce on small scales.

Can gravitational waves remember handedness?

In ghost inflation, parity-violating interactions can distinguish a configuration of scalar fluctuations from its mirror image. We traced this asymmetry from the scalar four-point function to the waves induced after inflation. For the scale-invariant case we studied, the effect was small, but it gave the left- and right-handed waves different power. That difference carries information that the sum over polarizations can hide.
